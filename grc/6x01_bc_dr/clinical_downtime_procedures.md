## Quick Reference 1: Epic EHR Downtime Procedures

**For:** Nurses, physicians, unit clerks and pharmacists. **Keep this sheet with the unit downtime binder.**

1. **Report and confirm the interruption.** If patient charts, medication records or orders cannot be accessed, tell the charge nurse immediately. The charge nurse contacts the Nursing Supervisor (extension **7100**). The Clinical Applications Lead (**7300**) confirms the application status with the IT Incident Commander (**7200**). **Only the Administrator on Call (7000) authorizes a hospital-wide downtime declaration.** The charge nurse tells staff when to begin paper documentation. Do not wait for a hospital-wide declaration to escalate an immediate patient-safety concern.

2. **Find the paper forms.** In the ICU, use the **red “Downtime Forms” binder at the charge nurse desk**; paper medication administration packets are in the **locked lower drawer under the charge nurse workstation**, and allergy cards are in the **wall pocket next to the medication room door**. In the Emergency Department, find the binder at the **charge station, cabinet 3, top shelf**; triage forms are in the **triage desk left drawer** and medication forms in the **Pyxis room wall file**. On the Medical-Surgical Unit, use the **blue binder rack at the central nursing station**; physician order sheets are at the **unit clerk desk, right cabinet**, and medication forms are on the **medication-room shelf above the barcode scanner docking station**.

3. **Protect medication safety.** **Verify allergies using an authorized reliable source before giving medication; never assume that a missing electronic record means no allergy.** Use the paper medication administration record to document the medication, dose, route, time and staff member. For uncertain allergies, doses or orders, **hold the non-emergency administration and escalate for clinical review**. Contact the charge nurse and pharmacist on duty (**7400**), then the attending physician as needed. Follow the hospital's emergency clinical protocols for time-critical treatment.

4. **Record care as it happens.** Physicians use paper physician order sheets; nurses use clinical assessment sheets for vital signs and observations. Document verbal orders on the **Verbal Order Read-Back Form** and perform a read-back. Write clearly, include patient identifiers, date, time and the author of each entry. Keep records secure and together with the patient's downtime packet.

5. **Escalate unresolved issues.** Follow the chain: **primary nurse → charge nurse → Nursing Supervisor (7100) → attending physician → Administrator on Call (7000)**. The laminated escalation phone list is attached to the ICU downtime binder. For medication-specific concerns, involve the pharmacist (**7400**) before the attending physician.

6. **Reconcile after restoration.** Resume electronic charting only after the responsible teams announce that the system is ready. The charge nurse and unit clerk use the **Downtime Reconciliation Cover Sheet** to track every paper order, medication entry, assessment and verbal order. **Check for missing or duplicate orders and medication administrations**, confirm discrepancies with the responsible clinician, and retain paper originals according to hospital records policy. The charge nurse verifies completion and reports any unresolved patient-safety issues.

## Quick Reference 2: Laboratory Information System Downtime Procedures

**For:** Ordering clinicians, nurses, laboratory accessioning staff and laboratory technicians. **Keep this sheet at the laboratory accessioning desk.**

1. **Report the outage and activate paper handling.** If laboratory orders or results cannot be entered or retrieved, notify the laboratory lead and the Nursing Supervisor (**7100**). The Clinical Applications Lead (**7300**) confirms the system status with the IT Incident Commander (**7200**). **The Administrator on Call (7000) authorizes a hospital-wide downtime declaration.** Staff should follow the laboratory's approved local instructions for urgent work while the status is being confirmed.

2. **Collect the correct forms.** The laboratory's **yellow “LIS Downtime” binder** is at the **accessioning desk**. **STAT requisitions** are in **accessioning tray 1** and also in the **Emergency Department specimen collection cart, second drawer**. Routine requisitions are in **accessioning tray 2**. The **Manual Specimen Tracking Log** is kept at the accessioning desk. The **Critical Value Call Log** is on the **chemistry bench clipboard**. The **Specimen Rejection Log** is in **accessioning tray 3**.

3. **Identify and document each specimen.** **Never process an unlabeled or mismatched specimen as though its identity were confirmed.** Every request and specimen record must include **patient full name, medical record number (MRN), date of birth, collection date and time, collector initials, requested test and ordering provider**. Record receipt and handoffs in the manual tracking log. Put identity problems in the rejection log and contact the ordering team for a safe recollection or other approved resolution.

4. **Prioritize urgent testing.** Mark urgent requests **STAT** on the paper requisition and alert laboratory accessioning (**7500**). If an urgent order cannot be located, processed or communicated, the **ordering clinician** contacts accessioning (**7500**), then the **chemistry lead (7510)**, then the **laboratory medical director (7520)**. Document who was contacted and when. 

5. **Communicate critical results directly.** **Call the ordering unit promptly for a critical value; do not rely on a paper form being seen.** Ask the receiver to **read back the result**. On the chemistry bench Critical Value Call Log, document the result, patient identifiers, receiver name, call time and read-back. **If nobody answers within 5 minutes, escalate to the charge nurse; if still unanswered at 10 minutes, escalate to the Nursing Supervisor (7100).** Continue the clinical escalation process until the result reaches an appropriate responsible clinician.

6. **Keep an auditable paper trail.** Record each test request, specimen receipt, rejection, result communication and correction with date, time and staff identification. Protect paper forms from unauthorized access. Keep logs organized to prevent patient mix-ups and duplicate entry.

7. **Reconcile when service returns.** Wait for confirmation that the laboratory system is ready before resuming normal entry. **Match every paper requisition and manual specimen entry to the correct patient and result**, identify missing tests, confirm critical-value calls and resolve duplicates with the ordering team. Record which paper items were entered, by whom and when. Preserve the paper records under hospital policy; escalate unresolved discrepancies to the laboratory medical director (**7520**) and Nursing Supervisor (**7100**).
