# Stakeholder Map

## Talking Point – Dr. Morales (CEO)

**Primary Concern:** Patient safety and organisational reputation.

Crimson Tide is no longer a theoretical cyber threat. A successful ransomware attack could interrupt access to electronic health records, delay clinical treatment and directly affect patient safety. Approving the emergency response plan protects patients, maintains public confidence and demonstrates that MedDefense is acting responsibly during an active healthcare threat.

---

## Talking Point – Robert Kim (CFO)

**Primary Concern:** Financial exposure, return on investment and cost of inaction.

The updated Annualised Loss Expectancy (ALE) is approximately **$400,000**, while renewing the FortiGate support contract costs only **$2,400**. This is a highly favourable investment because preventing a single ransomware incident avoids recovery costs, operational disruption, legal expenses and regulatory penalties that would greatly exceed the emergency expenditure.

---

## Talking Point – Dr. Reeves (Board Chair)

**Primary Concern:** Professional recommendation and confidence level.

My professional recommendation is to approve the emergency funding request and execute the 72-hour response plan immediately. Based on the completed assessment, I have a **high level of confidence** that these recommendations address MedDefense's highest current risks and are supported by recognised frameworks, quantitative risk analysis and current threat intelligence.

---

## Talking Point – Thomas Wright (Former Banker)

**Primary Concern:** Comparison with financial sector security maturity.

Compared with organisations in the financial sector, MedDefense demonstrates good governance but lower technical maturity in areas such as network segmentation, encryption at rest and identity protection. Financial institutions typically implement stronger defence-in-depth controls, making them an appropriate benchmark for MedDefense's long-term cybersecurity programme.

---

## Talking Point – Maria Santos (Legal Counsel)

**Primary Concern:** HIPAA liability, breach notification and cyber insurance.

If Crimson Tide successfully compromises patient information, MedDefense may be required to comply with HIPAA breach notification obligations, regulatory investigations and potential legal action. Prompt remediation, documented risk management and evidence of reasonable security controls reduce legal exposure and strengthen the organisation's position with cyber insurance providers and regulators.
