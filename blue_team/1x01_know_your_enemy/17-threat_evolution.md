# Threat Evolution Assessment

---

# Scenario A – Clinical Trial Partnership

## New Threat Actors

The partnership increases the likelihood of **Nation-State APTs**, because proprietary medical research becomes valuable for espionage. Organized crime also remains interested due to the sensitive patient data involved.

## Changed Vectors

Phishing against researchers, supply chain attacks targeting research partners and attacks against the new research server become more relevant. Insider threats also increase because more users have access to sensitive research data.

## Shifted Priorities

Nation-State attacks move from **Low** to **High** priority. Ransomware remains the highest threat, while insider threats also become more important due to valuable research information.

## New Gaps

- Research server requires stronger access controls.
- Research data needs encryption and monitoring.
- International partner access introduces additional third-party risk.

## Net Assessment

**Threat exposure increases significantly** because MedDefense now stores valuable intellectual property in addition to patient records.

---

# Scenario B – Cloud-Based EHR Migration

## New Threat Actors

Supply chain attackers become more important because the cloud provider becomes part of MedDefense's security perimeter. Nation-State actors may also target the cloud provider instead of the hospital directly.

## Changed Vectors

On-premises server exploits become less relevant. Cloud account compromise, stolen credentials and SaaS misconfigurations become the primary attack vectors.

## Shifted Priorities

Supply chain attacks move higher in priority. Legacy server attacks decrease because ehr-srv-01 and ehr-db-01 are decommissioned.

## New Gaps

- Cloud identity management.
- SaaS configuration security.
- Vendor monitoring and cloud logging.
- Shared responsibility management.

## Net Assessment

**Threat exposure shifts rather than decreases**, moving from infrastructure risks to cloud identity and third-party risks.

---

# Scenario C – Public Disclosure of the Ransomware Incident

## New Threat Actors

Hacktivists become more interested due to increased public attention. Opportunistic attackers may also target MedDefense believing it has weak security.

## Changed Vectors

Phishing, brand impersonation, social engineering and DDoS attacks become more likely because attackers can exploit the hospital's damaged reputation.

## Shifted Priorities

Hacktivists move from **Low** to **Medium** priority. Social engineering threats also increase because employees may react emotionally to media attention.

## New Gaps

- Crisis communication planning.
- Brand monitoring.
- Enhanced security awareness training.
- Public-facing website and patient portal protection.

## Net Assessment

**Threat exposure increases**, mainly because MedDefense becomes a more visible and attractive target for opportunistic attackers and hacktivists.

---

# Overall Conclusion

The threat landscape changes whenever MedDefense changes its business, technology or public profile. Clinical research increases espionage risk, cloud migration shifts the focus toward identity and supply chain security, and public exposure attracts opportunistic attackers and hacktivists. This demonstrates that threat assessments must be updated continuously as the organization evolves.
