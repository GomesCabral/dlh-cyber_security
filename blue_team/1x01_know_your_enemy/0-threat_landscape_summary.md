# Healthcare Threat Landscape Summary

## 1. Threat Actor Overview

### Organized Crime / Ransomware Groups
- **Who they are:** Financially motivated cybercriminal groups operating Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS).
- **Primary motivation:** Financial gain through ransomware payments and the sale of stolen patient data.
- **Sophistication:** Medium to High. They use purchased access, commercial tools and coordinated attack methods.

### Nation-State Actors
- **Who they are:** Government-sponsored advanced persistent threat (APT) groups.
- **Primary motivation:** Steal research, intellectual property and strategic healthcare information.
- **Sophistication:** Very High. They use custom malware, zero-day exploits and maintain long-term access.

### Insider Threats
- **Who they are:** Employees, contractors or former staff who intentionally or unintentionally compromise security.
- **Primary motivation:** Negligence, financial gain, curiosity or sabotage.
- **Sophistication:** Low to Medium.

### Hacktivists
- **Who they are:** Politically or ideologically motivated attackers.
- **Primary motivation:** Publicity, protest or disruption.
- **Sophistication:** Low to Medium.

### Opportunistic / Unskilled Attackers
- **Who they are:** Script kiddies and automated internet scanners searching for vulnerable systems.
- **Primary motivation:** Easy compromise using known vulnerabilities.
- **Sophistication:** Low, but increasing due to AI-assisted attack tools.

---

# 2. Healthcare Targeting Logic

Healthcare organizations are attractive targets because clinical operations cannot tolerate long outages, making hospitals more likely to pay ransomware demands. Patient medical records have high black-market value because they support identity theft, insurance fraud and long-term financial abuse. Healthcare environments also contain many legacy systems and medical devices that cannot easily be patched or replaced, creating exploitable vulnerabilities. Finally, many hospitals have limited cybersecurity staff and budgets, making it easier for attackers to compromise and maintain access to critical systems.

---

# 3. Trend Analysis

### Trend 1 – Ransomware attacks are increasing and becoming more destructive.

Threat actors increasingly use double extortion by stealing patient data before encrypting systems. According to the intelligence sources, 73% of healthcare ransomware incidents now include data exfiltration before encryption.

### Trend 2 – Initial access is shifting toward internet-facing services and stolen credentials.

Public-facing applications, phishing campaigns and compromised credentials account for the majority of initial compromises. AI-assisted tools are also lowering the technical skills required to launch effective attacks.

# 4. MedDefense Relevance

| Threat Actor | Relevance to MedDefense |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Organized Crime / Ransomware | **Very High.** MedDefense matches the typical profile of a mid-sized hospital with regulated patient data and limited security resources. |
| Nation-State Actors | **Low.** MedDefense does not conduct pharmaceutical or clinical research, making it a less attractive target. |
| Insider Threats | **High.** Shared accounts, incomplete offboarding and shadow IT increase insider risk. |
| Hacktivists | **Low.** MedDefense has little political visibility, although its public website and patient portal remain potential targets. |
| Opportunistic / Unskilled Attackers | **High.** Existing examples, including the billing server crypto-miner, show that automated attacks already target exposed vulnerabilities. |
