## RACI Legend

- **R – Responsible:** Performs the work.
- **A – Accountable:** Final owner of the activity and decision.
- **C – Consulted:** Provides expertise before decisions are made.
- **I – Informed:** Receives updates about progress and outcomes.

# Part 2 – Role Definitions

## Data Owner

**Assigned Role:** Dept Heads (for example Dr. Patel for Cardiology and the Finance Director for Billing).

### Definition

The Data Owner is responsible for determining how business data is classified, who is authorized to access it and how it supports business operations.

### Why

Department Heads understand the value, sensitivity and operational importance of the information generated by their departments. They own the business risk associated with that data.

---

## Data Controller

**Assigned Role:** MedDefense Health Systems (Executive Management).

### Definition

The Data Controller determines why and how personal data is processed and is legally responsible for compliance with GDPR and healthcare privacy regulations.

### Why

Only MedDefense can determine the lawful purpose for processing patient and employee information.

---

## Data Processor

**Assigned Role:** Third-party providers (for example SecurePoint Consulting, Microsoft 365 and other contracted vendors).

### Definition

A Data Processor processes personal information on behalf of the Data Controller following contractual instructions.

### Why

Service providers process MedDefense information but do not determine why or how it should be used.

---

## Data Custodian (Data Steward)

**Assigned Role:** Sarah Park (IT Director) and the Infrastructure Team.

### Definition

The Data Custodian is responsible for implementing and maintaining the technical controls that protect organizational data.

### Responsibilities

- Backup administration
- Storage management
- Patch management
- Access control implementation
- Encryption
- Server administration
- Disaster recovery support

### Why

The IT department manages the infrastructure where information is stored and processed but does not own the information itself.

---

# Governance Ownership vs Technical Custody

Business ownership belongs to the **Data Owner (Dept Heads)**, who decides how information should be used, classified and protected according to business needs. Technical custody belongs to the **Data Custodian (IT Director and Infrastructure Team)**, who implements and operates the technical safeguards that protect that information. This separation ensures that business decisions remain with management while technical implementation remains with IT.

# Part 3 – The CISO Question

## Current Situation

The **CISO position is currently vacant** at MedDefense Health Systems.

James Chen currently serves as **Deputy CISO**, providing operational leadership for cybersecurity activities, but he does not have the executive authority, independence or strategic decision-making responsibility of a permanent Chief Information Security Officer.

## Consequences of the Vacant CISO Position

- Cybersecurity lacks executive representation at Board level.
- Security priorities may conflict with operational IT priorities.
- Strategic security decisions depend heavily on executive management.
- Risk acceptance decisions have no dedicated security executive.
- Long-term governance and security planning are more difficult.
- Regulatory oversight and Board reporting become less effective.

## Recommendation

MedDefense has two realistic options:

- **Option 1:** Hire a full-time Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).
- **Option 2:** Outsource the function to a virtual Chief Information Security Officer (vCISO).

### Recommended Option

MedDefense should adopt a **virtual CISO (vCISO)** during the next 12–24 months.

With an annual cybersecurity budget of approximately **$120,000**, hiring a full-time experienced CISO would consume a large portion of the available funding, reducing the resources available for vulnerability remediation, SIEM deployment, network segmentation and medical device security improvements.

A vCISO can provide strategic leadership, governance, Board reporting, policy development, risk management and regulatory guidance at a significantly lower cost while James Chen continues leading day-to-day security operations. As MedDefense's cybersecurity maturity increases and future budgets expand, the organization can transition to a permanent full-time CISO.

---

# Overall Governance Assessment

This governance architecture establishes clear separation between executive governance, cybersecurity leadership, IT operations and business ownership. Executive management owns business risk and strategic decisions, the Deputy CISO coordinates the cybersecurity program, the IT Director owns technical implementation, Department Heads own business data and operational impact, and the Security Analyst performs technical assessments, vulnerability management, monitoring and audit support. This structure aligns with NIST CSF 2.0 Govern (GV), CIS Controls v8 and ISO/IEC 27001 governance principles while remaining appropriate for MedDefense's size, staffing and budget.
